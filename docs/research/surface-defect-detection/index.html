An Adaptive Contrastive Learning-Based Robust Surface Defect Detection Model for Noisy Datasets

2026 · IEEE Access · Vol. 14, pp. 177782–177797 · First author

I studied how to detect surface defects when the training images include defective samples.

Read the paper

Why training data matters

Surface inspection data collected on a factory line can contain subtle defects even when it is assembled as a normal training set. A model trained on that mixture can learn to treat some defects as examples of normal appearance. This study tested image-level detection under controlled contamination.

Adaptive contrastive learning

As first author, I worked on a self-supervised contrastive approach that tests individual samples using a one-sample t-test. The p-value feeds an adjustment to the temperature of the contrastive loss. That changes how strongly the learning objective treats similarities when the training examples are less trustworthy.

What we evaluated

We varied the share of defective training images and evaluated the detector on Surface Crack, Magnetic Tile, and KolektorSDD2. We compared the method with self-supervised and patch-based baselines under the same contamination conditions. The IEEE Access article reports the full setup and results.
