An Adaptive Contrastive Learning-Based Robust Surface Defect Detection Model for Noisy Datasets

2026 · IEEE Access · Vol. 14, pp. 177782–177797 · First author

A training set labeled normal can already contain defects. I studied how to train an image-level detector under those conditions.

Read the paper

Choosing a temperature for each positive pair

We generate two views of each training image and compare how similar they are with the cosine similarities those views have to the other samples in the mini-batch. We use a two-sided one-sample t-test to decide whether InfoNCE should use a low or high temperature for that pair. The low setting increases the influence of hard negatives during training.

Scoring new images with nearest neighbors

After training, we use the encoder features before the projection layer to score new images with k-nearest neighbors. The detector assigns a score to the whole image. Finding the exact location of a defect would require a separate evaluation at the pixel or patch level.

Fixed, random, and adaptive settings

On Surface Crack and Magnetic Tile, the rule gave a higher mean ROC AUC than holding the temperature fixed or switching randomly. The paper also evaluates image-level detection on KolektorSDD2. Similarities computed from the same mini-batch share samples, so we use the p-value as a training rule.
